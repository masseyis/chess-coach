Coordinate a king attack

Successful attacks involve time (lead in development), force (several pieces), and targets (weak squares around the king). A lone sacrificial idea seldom works without backup. If the engine critiques your attack, you probably rushed before bringing enough pieces or ignored your opponent's counter threats.

Checklist before launching an attack

  • Count attackers vs. defenders: aim for three pieces near the enemy king before sacrificing.
  • Open lines toward the king with pawn breaks (f-pawn for kingside attacks, c/f for queenside opposite-side castling).
  • Keep your own king safe—connect rooks and control back-rank squares before pushing pawns in front of your monarch.
  • Look for forcing moves that gain tempo (checks and threats) so the opponent cannot regroup.

Practice ideas

  • Replay classic attacking games by Tal or Kasparov and narrate the attacking checklist move by move.
  • Set up common sacrifices (Greek Gift, rook lift) against the engine and practice calculating follow-ups.
  • Alternate sides in training—defend against the same attack to understand the resources you must stop.
